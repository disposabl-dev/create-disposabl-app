# Subjective design review

These are design hypotheses, evaluated from the written compositions and generated HTML, not observed user tests. No conversion, preference, or performance metrics are claimed. All previews are illustrative and should be checked visually in target browsers before production. The five compositions change ordering and hierarchy; the family-specific CSS changes the platform graphic, typography, and visual grammar.

Criteria: clarity of promise and action, distinctiveness, credibility, responsive feasibility, and accessibility.

1. **001 — Launch Sheet / Front Page (recommended):** Strong typographic hierarchy, command beside a clear platform proof, and an honest cost note. The most balanced candidate for production. Risk: the long ownership headline needs careful wrapping on small screens.
2. **002 — Launch Sheet / Split Desk:** A balanced two-column composition keeps the platform proof visible. Risk: less room for long copy.
3. **004 — Launch Sheet / Command Stage:** Makes the next action a primary object. Risk: the headline-to-action separation needs visual verification.
4. **056 — Switchboard / Front Page:** A tactile platform panel gives the concept a recognizable identity. Risk: must not suggest that the depicted platforms are interactive switches.
5. **096 — Manifesto / Front Page:** The ownership statement has conviction and legibility. Risk: platform evidence competes with the claim.
6. **016 — Prism / Front Page:** Layered forms make the multi-platform idea visually distinctive. Risk: skewed cards could impair scanability.
7. **021 — Blueprint / Front Page:** The grid supports a credible technical foundation story. Risk: the engineering aesthetic may narrow appeal.
8. **076 — Split Reality / Front Page:** Input and platform outcome are easy to relate. Risk: avoid implying the illustrative panel is a generated result.
9. **051 — Receipt / Front Page:** A cost ledger reinforces transparency. Risk: the visual is more utilitarian than aspirational.
10. **041 — App Gallery / Front Page:** Device silhouettes help explain platform reach. Risk: they are schematic rather than genuine app examples.
